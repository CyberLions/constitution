\section*{Article I -- Name and Affiliation}

\textbf{Name}

The official name of this organization shall be the Pennsylvania State University -- Competitive Cyber Security Organization, known herein as CCSO.

\textbf{Affiliation}

CCSO is not affiliated with any national organization; it is local to the University Park campus.

\section*{Article II -- Mission Statement}

\textbf{Purpose}

The purpose of the Penn State Competitive Cyber Security Organization (CCSO) is to provide members with opportunities to pursue their interests in all facets of cybersecurity, develop their technical skills, collaborate with alumni and other organizations, and provide mentorship opportunities.

\textbf{Mission}

The mission of CCSO is to develop the technical skills and knowledge of our members and provide them with the opportunity to apply themselves by participating in various cybersecurity competitions.

\textbf{Goal}

The goal of CCSO is to develop our members and represent Penn State in cybersecurity competitions.

\section*{Article III -- Membership}

\begin{itemize}
  \item At all times, the majority (50\%+1) of all active members shall be full-time, officially registered students at University Park.
  \item The membership shall be divided into active and associate members.
  \item Only currently registered students who have paid club dues are eligible for active membership.
  \item All other members interested in dedicating themselves to the purpose of the Penn State Competitive Cyber Security Organization, including but not limited to, faculty, students from other Penn State campuses, staff, and community members, shall be associate members.
  \item Only active members may hold office, vote, preside, officiate, solicit funds on behalf of the organization, or represent the organization in competitions.
  \item At all times there shall be at least 10 active student members. A list of active members must be maintained by the organization.
  \item The club reserves the right to expel a member who is not following the policies of the organization with a super-majority vote (75\%) of attending members.
  \item All members, including but not limited to new members and recruits, reserve the right to refrain from participating in any activities without consequence, based upon personal/religious beliefs, personal values, or moral reserve as defined by the member.
  \item Any interaction/activity between members and/or new members will be limited to guidelines stated by university policy, as well as local, state, and federal laws.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{New Members and Recruitment}

Any new members will be given full disclosure during recruitment, including but not limited to the disclosure of:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item New Membership Manual (if applicable).
  \item Schedule of New Member Events and Activities (if applicable).
  \item A list of all responsibilities.
  \item A copy of the University Hazing Policy, prescribed by Policies and Rules for Student Organizations. This document is available upon request by the Office of Student Activities.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Non-Hazing Compliance Statement}

The CCSO and The Pennsylvania State University does not tolerate hazing. Hazing is prohibited for any University recognized or sanctioned organization, student, or other person associated with an organization operating under the sanction of or recognized by the University.

Organizations or individuals found responsible for hazing under this Policy, whether occurring on or off campus, will be subject to disciplinary action by the University, and may also face criminal charges under state law, including The Timothy J. Piazza Antihazing Law, 18 Pa. C.S. \S~2801, et seq.

Annually, the CCSO will share the University Hazing Policy with the entire membership, prescribed by Policies and Rules for Student Organizations.

\textbf{Non-Discrimination Statement}

CCSO, following the University's Non-Discrimination Policy, will not discriminate membership based on any protected class outlined at Penn State Policy AD-91.

\textbf{Membership Mediation}

In the case of violations of constitutional policies and/or a conflict between members within the organization, the highest-ranking officer that is not involved in the conflict, with assistance from the advisor (if applicable), will facilitate an informal mediation procedure:

The mediator shall:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Arrange for a mediation meeting outside of the regular organization business meeting.
  \item Explain their role as the impartial party and the objectives of the mediation.
  \item Set ground rules.
  \item Allow each party to express their views by allowing the conversation to go where the parties wish it to go.
  \item Collect any available resources that might assist in the resolution (financial documents, emails, photos, etc.).
  \item As a third party, do not suggest resolutions but rather leave the responsibility for the resolution with the parties involved.
  \item Facilitate goal setting to reach a win-win resolution.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Membership Removal}

The club reserves the right to expel a member who is not following the policies of the organization with a super-majority vote (75\%) of attending members.

\section*{Article IV -- Officers}

In accordance with University policy, co-President and co-Treasurer positions are not permitted; the President and Treasurer positions may not be held by the same student at the same time.

\begin{itemize}
  \item Undergraduate candidates must be full-time, officially registered active student members to be selected as an officer, captain, or chair at the time of the appointment or election. Graduate candidates may be full-time or part-time, officially registered active student members to be selected as an officer, captain, or chair at the time of the appointment or election. Officers, captains, and chairs may change from full-time to part-time, or vice versa, after starting their role. The organization may establish and apply additional eligibility criteria for appointed or elected leaders/officers upon a super-majority (75\%) vote.
  \item Active members may be elected to officer positions according to the rules in the Election of Officers section below.
  \item The Officers should meet at least once a week when possible. This should include captains and chairs for at least a portion of the meeting.
  \item The Officers should meet with the advisor at least three (3) times a semester to coordinate efforts and discuss opportunities.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Executive Officers}

\textit{President (required position)}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item The President is the spokesperson of the organization and the primary contact for all external communication. The President may delegate communication as they see fit.
  \item The President defines the vision and goals that the club will work towards during their term.
  \item The President is responsible for leading the organization and directing efforts.
  \item The goal of the President will be to ensure the organization is serving its members.
\end{enumerate}

\textit{Vice-President}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item The Vice-President shall serve as the President when the President is unable to do so.
  \item The Vice-President shall serve as the Tech Director when the position is vacant.
  \item The Vice-President will preside over the Offensive and Defensive captains to support the competitive teams. Responsibilities include tracking registration dates, facilitating travel, and oversight of activities.
  \item The goal of the Vice-President will be to support the President and to ensure the long-term success of the organization.
\end{enumerate}

\textit{Treasurer (required position)}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item The Treasurer will be responsible for all organizational finances and will keep an up-to-date budget.
  \item The Treasurer will be responsible for all UPAC and ASA meetings and interactions.
  \item The Treasurer will collect and track membership dues each semester.
  \item The Treasurer will oversee the corporate engagement program, work closely with the Sponsorship Chair, and maintain a list of affiliated corporations.
  \item The Treasurer will plan and book reservations for all club travel unless otherwise decided by the President and Vice-President.
  \item The goal of the Treasurer will be to keep the organization financially stable and to fully benefit from all available University funding and resources.
\end{enumerate}

\textit{Secretary}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item The Secretary is responsible for ensuring all organizational needs are met. This includes but is not limited to sending bi-weekly announcements, maintaining OrgCentral, and updating organizational documents.
  \item The Secretary is responsible for yearly club merchandise orders and distribution. This includes but is not limited to conducting interest surveys, defining pricing, placing the order, and delivering merchandise.
  \item The Secretary shall serve as the Competition Chair when the position is vacant.
  \item The goal of the Secretary will be to enhance engagement and ensure our members are benefiting from the organization.
\end{enumerate}

\textit{Event Planner (required position)}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item The Event Planner will lead the Social Team and is responsible for planned activities excluding competitions, including but not limited to booking university spaces and organizing participation in involvement fairs.
  \item The Event Planner will maintain a master schedule for the academic year listing all events.
  \item The Event Planner will be responsible for facilitating the Involvement Fair each semester. This includes but is not limited to scheduling people to be present at the table, creating the posterboard, and facilitating recruiting activities.
  \item The Event Planner shall serve as the Recruitment and Social chair when the positions are vacant.
  \item The goal of the Event Planner will be to plan and support organization events, outreach and community engagement.
\end{enumerate}

\textit{Tech Director}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item The Tech Director will lead the Tech Team and is responsible for the overall strategy and management of all technical resources within the organization. This includes but is not limited to the organization's communication channels, club domain names, and club infrastructure.
  \item The Tech Director will facilitate the technological resources for any club activities.
  \item The Tech Director will serve as the Webmaster when the position is vacant.
  \item The goal of the Tech Director will be managing and maintaining the technical resources of the club to facilitate trainings, competitions, and club image.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Captains}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Following elections and at least one week prior to the end of the Spring semester, the Executive Board shall appoint the Offensive Captain, Defensive Captain, and CTF Captain for the upcoming academic year by a majority vote.
  \item The Executive Board shall solicit recommendations for new Captains from the outgoing Captains, and shall also accept statements of interest from active members.
  \item Newly appointed captains fully assume their roles and responsibilities at the end of the Spring semester.
  \item Active members may be appointed to captain positions by a majority vote of the Executive Board.
  \item The President may appoint active members to a vacant captain position without a vote with consent from the Vice-President.
  \item Active members may hold a single officer or captain position at a time.
  \item Captains may hold an Officer position with the approval of both the current President and club Advisor.
\end{itemize}

\textit{Offensive, Defensive, and CTF Captains}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Each captain position can only be held by one individual. No one may hold multiple captain positions simultaneously.
  \item The Offensive and Defensive Captains will lead the CPTC and CCDC competition teams respectively, as well as any other non-CTF cybersecurity competitions unless otherwise decided by the President.
  \item The CTF Captain will identify and promote CTF competitions throughout the year, select teams, and organize practices or delegate the organization of practices to individual teams.
  \item The CTF Captain will organize SillyCTF, an annual public CTF competition in the Spring semester, with support from the Executive Board. The CTF Captain may delegate this task.
  \item The CTF Captain will organize The Conspiracy, an annual Alternate Reality Game in the Fall semester, with support from the Executive Board. The CTF Captain may delegate this task.
  \item The Offensive and Defensive Captains will hold open meetings focused on their respective cybersecurity topics at least three (3) times a semester.
  \item The Offensive and Defensive Captains must hold tryouts for the CPTC and CCDC competitive teams before the end of September.
  \item The Captains must have a detailed training plan prior to the start of the semester that must be approved by the President and Vice-President.
  \item The goal of the captain positions will be to gain hands-on leadership experience in a security-focused environment and represent Penn State at various competitive events.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Chairs}

\begin{itemize}
  \item The President may appoint active members to a chair position without a vote with consent from the Vice-President.
  \item The President may create, modify, or dismiss chair positions without a vote with consent from the Vice-President.
  \item There is no limit on the number of chair positions that can be created. In addition to individual chair roles, formal teams may be established to manage specific club functions.
  \item There are no restrictions on the number of chair positions a member can hold.
  \item Responsibilities of Officers can be delegated to chair positions as needed, within reason. Created chair positions should report to their respective officer.
  \item Disputes over unfair delegation or workloads should follow the informal mediation process.
  \item The constitutional list of chair positions is non-exhaustive.
  \item Filling vacancies: If there is a sudden vacancy for an officer position, nominations must be taken at the meeting following the vacancy. Refer to the rules in the Election of Officers section below.
\end{itemize}

\textit{Tech Team}

The Tech Team is responsible for the design, implementation, and maintenance of all club technical infrastructure. This includes systems supporting competitions, educational resources, and the club's online presence. The team works collaboratively to provide a stable technical environment for all members.

The Tech Team is led by the Tech Director, see Tech Director role documentation in the Executive Officers section above. All team members report to the Tech Director.

Team Roles and Responsibilities:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Cloud Systems Administrator}
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item Manages and maintains all cloud-based infrastructure, primarily utilizing platforms such as Kubernetes, Oracle Cloud, and Rancher.
    \item Responsible for cloud service provisioning, identity and access management (IAM), and managing the club's domain names and DNS records.
  \end{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{On-Premises Systems Administrator}
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item Manages and maintains all physical and locally hosted club infrastructure, including servers, networking equipment, and virtualization platforms (e.g., OpenStack, Kubernetes, Ansible).
    \item Responsible for hardware maintenance, operating system patching, and ensuring the availability of on-premises lab environments.
  \end{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Webmaster}
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item Maintains the organization's public-facing online platforms, including the official website, social media accounts, and the Discord server.
    \item Responsible for content updates, theme management, and ensuring a positive user experience on all club platforms.
  \end{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{CTF Creation Chair}
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item Leads the development, testing, and deployment of Capture The Flag (CTF) challenges for use in club meetings, workshops, and recruitment events.
    \item Works closely with the Systems Administrators to deploy challenges on the appropriate infrastructure.
  \end{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Resource Developer}
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item Creates and curates technical and educational content for the club, such as workshops, resources, and documentation.
    \item Manages the club's knowledge base (e.g., wiki or Git repository) to ensure resources are organized and accessible to all members.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{itemize}

\textit{Social Team}

The Social Team manages recruitment, internal events, public branding, and corporate relationships to ensure the club's sustained growth and engagement.

The Social Team is led by the Event Planner, see Event Planner role documentation in the Executive Officers section above. All team members report to the Event Planner, though the Sponsorship Chair will also work closely with the Treasurer and President.

Team Roles and Responsibilities:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Sponsorship Chair}
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item Actively seeks out and communicates with potential corporate sponsors and industry partners to secure funding, resources, and guest speakers for the organization.
    \item Maintains relationships with existing sponsors and coordinates closely with the Treasurer to manage incoming corporate funds and compliance with university policies.
    \item Documents contacts within the Wiki as needed.
  \end{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Social Chair}
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item The Social Chair shall plan, organize, and execute at least 2 social and fundraising events per semester.
    \item The Social Chair will report to the Event Planner unless otherwise decided by the President.
  \end{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Recruitment Chair}
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item The Recruitment Chair shall plan and organize recruitment events such as the Involvement Fair.
    \item The Recruitment Chair will report to the Event Planner unless otherwise decided by the President.
  \end{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Social Media Design Chair}
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item Creates high-quality digital and print graphics, flyers, and promotional materials for club events, meetings, and announcements.
    \item Manages the club's branding and ensures engaging content is posted across all social media platforms regularly.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Election of Officers}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Officer elections shall take place during a regularly scheduled meeting more than a month prior to the end of the Spring semester.
  \item Newly elected officers fully assume their roles and responsibilities at the end of the Spring semester.
  \item This meeting shall be publicized at least 1 month in advance and in the meetings preceding it and through all organizational communication platforms such as Discord and email.
\end{itemize}

Election codes:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Nominees for the role of President must have previously held an Officer, Captain, or Chair role in the organization for at least 1 semester. Should no one meet these criteria, any active member may be nominated.
  \item All active members may cast a single vote per office.
  \item Any member may nominate an active member for an office prior to the election. Candidates must accept their nomination prior to elections.
  \item Candidates must prepare a brief presentation in support of their candidacy, and answer questions from members.
  \item Members must be present to vote.
  \item The specific format for elections and nominations may be determined by the President with majority consent from the Executive Board.
  \item If a simple majority (50\%+1) cannot be obtained by a single candidate, the candidate with the lowest number of votes shall be dropped from consideration and the active members shall vote again.
  \item In the event of a tie, the ballots will be recast.
  \item No one involved in conducting the elections may be an official candidate.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Officer Removal}

\begin{itemize}
  \item In the event an officer or captain is not fulfilling their duties as outlined by the President and in the Officers or Captains sections respectively, they meet grounds for dismissal from their position.
  \item The President and Vice-President may issue a verbal or written warning outlining the officer or captain in question's failed duties and other misconduct as applicable.
  \item After a warning is issued, the officer or captain in question will begin a two-week probationary period where they can address the grievances cited in the warning.
  \item If after the probationary period, the officer or captain in question is still not meeting the expectations outlined in the warning, the Executive Board and chairs can vote to remove the officer or captain using a two-thirds majority.
  \item If the officer or captain in question believes any part of this proceeding to be unfair or unfounded, these disputes can be discussed with the club advisor.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Officer Replacement}

In the event an officer or captain steps down, or a position is vacant, the following steps should be taken to replace the officer or captain:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item The officer or captain stepping down must provide a minimum of two weeks' notice that they are stepping down.
  \item Upon notice of an officer stepping down, the Executive Board must begin electing a replacement officer using the rules in the Election of Officers section above.
  \item Upon notice of a captain stepping down, the Executive Board must appoint a new captain as defined in the Captains section above.
  \item An interim officer or captain may be appointed by the President and Vice-President as needed until the officer or captain position is officially filled.
\end{enumerate}

\section*{Article V -- Operating Procedure}

\textbf{Meetings}

\begin{itemize}
  \item The Penn State Competitive Cyber Security Organization will meet at least once per month.
  \item Adequate advance notice about the date, time, and location of the meeting must be given to all active members.
  \item The President, with the consent of the Vice-President, may change the meeting frequency.
  \item The President may call special meetings at their discretion.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Decision-Making Process}

\begin{itemize}
  \item A quorum for all voting shall be thirty-three percent (33\%) of the active members. If an active member does not attend four (4) meetings in a row or misses two meetings with votes, they are not counted when forming a quorum.
  \item Only active members may vote.
  \item A simple majority (50\%+1) of the present, active members is required for a vote to succeed unless otherwise stated.
  \item The President shall not cast a vote unless it is a tie-breaking vote.
  \item The method and format for voting may be determined by the President with majority consent from the Executive Board.
  \item The method and format for voting must be reasonable and cannot be used to exclude active members from voting.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Amending the Constitution}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Amendments to this constitution may be introduced at any time. Voting must take place at least a week after an amendment's introduction.
  \item Any active member can introduce an amendment.
  \item Voting shall occur at the meeting following an amendment's introduction. Prior to voting, there must be a discussion about the proposed changes.
  \item Upon a successful vote, the amendment takes effect following Office of Student Activities approval.
  \item The organization's advisor must be notified of proposed constitution changes at least a week in advance and withholds the right to veto constitutional amendments.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Parliamentary Authority}

Robert's Rules of Order, Newly Revised, by Sarah Corbin Roberts shall be used in all cases not covered by this constitution.

\textbf{Accessibility of this Constitution}

Copies of this constitution shall be made available to anyone upon request.

\section*{Article VI -- Advisors}

\begin{itemize}
  \item This organization must always retain at least one advisor. The advisor will be a full-time Faculty or Staff member of The Pennsylvania State University, University Park campus and will be chosen by the organization.
  \item This organization will choose an advisor through a majority vote.
  \item The Advisor shall not vote as an active member and shall solely act at the discretion of the Executive Board.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Duties of the Advisor}
\begin{itemize}
  \item The Advisor shall serve as an official representative for the Penn State Competitive Cyber Security Organization when required by a competition.
  \item The Advisor shall serve as an intermediary between the Penn State Competitive Cyber Security Organization and The Pennsylvania State University or the College of Information Sciences and Technology.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Article VII -- Financial Statement}

\textbf{Finances}
\begin{itemize}
  \item All organizational funds are to be deposited and handled exclusively through the Associated Student Activities (ASA).
  \item This organization will not have an off-campus account(s).
  \item This organization will collect reasonable dues if and only if:
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item The dues are not excessive.
    \item There is a proven need for dues as income for the organization.
    \item Dues are determined by the Treasurer with consent from the President.
  \end{enumerate}
  \item Dues shall be collected semi-annually.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Spending}
\begin{itemize}
  \item The use of organizational funds for competition travel, preparation, registration, or other uses must be approved by either the Treasurer or the majority of the Executive Board.
  \item All other uses of organizational funds require both Treasurer approval and majority consent from the Executive Board.
  \item If a dispute about the use of organizational funds cannot be resolved, follow the outlined informal mediation process.
\end{itemize}

This Constitution was reviewed and ratified by the Executive Board on September 27, 2026, at a regular body meeting.

The entire membership and Executive Board have reviewed and agree to this document.

\section*{Additional Procedures}

\textbf{Competition Teams}

\textit{Tryouts}
\begin{itemize}
  \item The Offensive and Defensive Captains must hold tryouts for the CPTC and CCDC competitive teams before the end of September.
  \item Each tryout should consist of a hands-on exercise that provides a fair assessment of skills required to be a successful member of the competition team.
  \item Captains may solicit help from other members, alumni, or associate members to build the tryout exercise. If possible, only members not planning to participate in tryouts should assist with the creation of the exercise. Otherwise, another fair evaluation must be provided by the captain for that member.
  \item The result of these tryouts may be used to determine the makeup of other competition teams, such as CyberForce. It's encouraged to hold a tryout exercise for all competitions involving travel.
\end{itemize}

\textit{Team Selection}
\begin{itemize}
  \item For competitions that do not have an associated tryout, captains may use an application form to select their teams.
  \item Team captains have the final say over team selection.
  \item If a competition has alternate members, the team captain is free to determine the process of selecting both alternates and full members. This process must be made clear in advance to all involved team members, ideally at tryouts/application.
  \item Captains must confer with the advisor and the highest level Executive Board member not seeking a spot on the team to confirm their team selections.
  \item Teams should be selected in a fair manner that emphasizes the skills required to be a successful member of the competition team.
\end{itemize}

\textit{Advisor Approval}

Any competition that requires or references advisor involvement, whether for travel or virtual participation, must receive advisor approval prior to registration. The advisor must be informed of their role and responsibilities before registration is completed. No member may sign on behalf of an advisor or submit documentation bearing an advisor's name without their explicit written consent. Failure to follow this process will be reviewed by the organization's advisor, who will determine appropriate corrective action, which may include removal from the team, removal from the leadership position, or further administrative action.
